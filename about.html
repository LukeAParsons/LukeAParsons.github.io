About

Background

I grew up backpacking in the canyons and mountains of Arizona and Utah, and a high school geology teacher in Albuquerque turned that into an interest in Earth science. I studied geology and Spanish at Brown University, where my senior thesis used ancient leaf waxes in lake sediments to reconstruct past climate. After college I worked as a hydrology intern with the US Geological Survey, then taught middle and high school science, math, and Spanish for five years outside Boston.

Summers spent instructing NOLS courses in Wyoming's Wind River Range brought me back to research. Comparing the glaciers in front of me with older topographic maps made the changes hard to ignore, and I returned to graduate school to study drought and climate at the University of Arizona as an NSF Graduate Research Fellow, with fieldwork in New Mexico, Nepal, Ecuador, and Peru. I then held a Washington Research Foundation Postdoctoral Fellowship at the University of Washington and was a research scientist and lecturer at Duke University before joining the Global Science team at The Nature Conservancy in 2023. I also hold an adjunct appointment in Atmospheric Sciences at the University of Utah.

Approach and tools

My work integrates climate model projections (CMIP6), reanalysis datasets (ERA5), and observations with biophysical and health models and with socioeconomic and vulnerability indicators. My expertise includes data assimilation, uncertainty quantification, climate risk and impact assessment, and applied statistical analysis. I work mainly in Python (Xarray) and bash on large climate datasets and model output. I am fluent in Spanish.

Teaching and mentoring

At Duke's Nicholas School of the Environment, I was the primary lecturer for Climate and Society, a course for undergraduate and graduate students covering climate basics, projections, and societal choices. I have guest lectured at the University of Washington and the University of Utah, and from 2019 to 2025 I taught a Master Naturalist class for adults on climate basics and warming impacts in New Mexico. Earlier, I taught AP Environmental Science, geology, and Spanish at the middle and high school level and led 30-day backpacking courses for NOLS. I have mentored students at the University of Arizona, the University of Washington, and Duke, and I mentor scientists at The Nature Conservancy.

Beyond research

I am a landscape photographer; my work received a Silver Medal in the 2012 Epson International Photography Awards, and you can see more at LukeParsonsPhoto.com. Hiking, trail running, backpacking, and climbing are how I connect with the landscapes I study, and I have been a Wilderness First Responder since 2011.
